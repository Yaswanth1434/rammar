Digital Devices

Digital devices represent information with discrete logic states and process, store, or communicate digital data. The examples below show common device types and representative items; exact functions vary by part.

Microcontrollers (MCUs) and processors
Run program instructions and control peripherals. Representative items: STM32 development boards, Microchip PIC microcontrollers, and single-board computers using application processors.
Digital logic ICs
Perform Boolean operations, signal selection, counting, and state control. Items include logic gates, buffers, multiplexers, decoders, flip-flops, counters, and shift registers.
Memory devices
Store firmware or data. Items include SRAM for fast temporary storage, EEPROM for rewritable non-volatile data, and Flash memory for firmware, removable storage, or mass storage.
Programmable logic
Implement custom digital logic. Items include CPLDs and FPGAs, used for parallel processing, timing control, digital signal interfaces, and custom hardware logic.
Digital interface and communication ICs
Translate or manage signals between digital systems. Items include USB controllers, Ethernet controllers, CAN transceivers, and UART, SPI, or I²C interface devices.

Materials commonly found in digital devices and assemblies

Semiconductor die
Primarily silicon in mainstream logic, processor, and memory ICs; other semiconductor materials may be used in specialized devices.
IC package
Typically includes a molded polymer or ceramic body, a copper-alloy lead frame or package substrate, and conductive interconnects. The exact construction depends on the package and part.
Printed circuit board (PCB)
Common rigid boards use FR-4 glass-fiber/epoxy laminate with copper traces, solder mask, and a surface finish. Flexible assemblies may use polyimide; higher-frequency designs may use other laminates.
Assembly materials
Include solder alloy, component leads or pads, connectors, and insulation. Choose materials and processes for the board, temperature, current, environment, and applicable safety or environmental requirements.

These are typical examples, not a bill of materials for every digital device. Check the exact component datasheet and PCB/assembly documentation for material, package, and compatibility details.
